Spring 2027 dry-run review
=========================

This is the decision register for department chairs and the college scheduling administrator. The posted schedule is evidence of actual practice; catalog worksheets and descriptions supply degree requirements, not a complete description of which students take courses together. Import rules are provisional inferences. A consistent rule can still produce an inappropriate constraint, so review the resulting meetings and conflicts as well as the rule itself.

`AGENTS.md` describes the input pipeline; `../NOTES.md` describes current behavior and separate future design proposals. The Python input files and `schema.sql` determine the current model. Archives are reference material, not current departmental approval. No questions below have been answered by chairs unless explicitly marked as a confirmed correction.


Administrator decisions
-----------------------

1. Decide which concurrent student paths deserve protection. A degree-wide requirement list currently creates pairwise pressure between courses at different stages unless a prerequisite path removes it. Ask chairs for expected concurrent groups, de-facto ordering, and acceptable exceptions rather than treating every required course as concurrently needed. Decide whether omitted non-college courses need fixed meetings or faculty availability blocks to protect college scheduling.
2. Review the elective-pool decisions below. Broad pools where students select a modest fraction, especially across departments, contribute no pressure. Focused requirements can remain modeled. Numeric selection ratios alone do not settle this: a focused science or mathematics requirement differs from free choices across many programs. Keep a few meaningful curriculum tiers; do not turn every possible choice into a weak conflict.
3. Decide what evidence makes a posted time genuinely fixed. Current importers often fix a meeting because its pattern is not recognized, without evidence that it cannot move. Generous faculty availability does not help a singleton section time domain. Review the fixed-time findings below before interpreting solver minima as unavoidable institutional constraints.
4. Approve operational assumptions about teaching space, shared meetings, and instructors. College-owned SET/Snow/Smith rooms are the main pool; floated Brown/COE/GCB/UHB rooms remain options for their posted sections. Verify capacities, equipment, accessibility, ownership, and travel needs. Matching room/time/dates is evidence of a shared meeting, not proof; a mistaken merge imposes every listed instructor's constraints on the common meeting.
5. Decide the reporting standard for comparisons. Saved SAT snapshots can place every section while optimizing only an initial priority prefix. Separate proven optimized prefixes from later incidental penalties. Compare generated and posted meetings under the same current rules; distinguish pairwise overlap penalties from genuinely blocked student choices, fixed input contradictions, and model limitations. Chair acceptance of a posted overlap is evidence for investigation, not automatic authorization to suppress the pair.


Evidence from the initial runs
------------------------------

Before the November 10 curriculum corrections, all 17 saved placements contained all 474 scheduled sections; optimization progress differed. Placement 17 had 14 priority-1 conflicts, 18 at priority 3, four at priority 4, and 44 at priority 5. Applying the same conflict view to the posted meeting times gave 50, 25, five, and 55 respectively, plus three at priority 2. Seven of placement 17's priority-1 conflicts were unavoidable because both endpoint time domains were singletons. All seven already overlapped in the posting. One was the cross-listing defect described below; the other six need chair interpretation rather than an assumption that the solver should eliminate them.

All 44 priority-5 penalties in placement 17 came from broad elective cliques: 19 from Bioinformatics and 25 from engineering. These pools are now omitted. This is a diagnosis of the old snapshot, not a new optimum claim. New solver runs are needed after input changes.

Re-evaluating placement 17's saved coordinates under the revised conflict view gives 13 priority-1 conflicts, 14 at priority 3, none at priorities 4/5, 97 at priority 6, two at priority 7, and 19 at priority 8. The corrected posted timetable gives 46 at priority 1, three at priority 2, 21 at priority 3, two at priority 5, 156 at priority 6, one at priority 7, and 23 at priority 8. These are coordinate-based curriculum evaluations, not new optimized runs. The database rebuild clears saved placements.

The large introductory Biology lecture/lab offerings were not creating strong curriculum pressure: BIOL 1610/1615 had 3/9 student choices, BIOL 1620/1625 had 2/5, and BIOL 2320/2325 had 8/20. Their soft lecture/lab conflicts were discounted away. The remaining BIOL 1620/lab hard pair came from a shared instructor. Supplemental instruction contributed only two soft penalties in placement 17, both at priority 6.

Of 98 priority-6 penalties in placement 17, 76 had a nonoverlapping alternative section pairing or an untimed offering. That check did not establish capacity, registration eligibility, or compatibility with a student's other courses. It shows why a hallpass count is not a count of students unable to complete a requirement.

Fixed-time import review
------------------------

The initial database had 167 single-slot sections: Biological Sciences 56, Engineering/Physics 33, Chemistry 23, Mathematics 17, Earth Sciences 17, Computing 14, and Design seven. Seventy were entirely within Monday–Friday 09:00–16:30; the rest touched earlier or later hours. No singleton resulted from faculty availability reducing a larger tagged time set to one. The import rules or time-tag definitions themselves supplied only one concrete choice. These numbers describe the initial import, not an approved target.

1. Three-credit lecture importers use exact posted-slot whitelists rather than recognizing all valid patterns. Examples left fixed include PHYS 1010-01 and MATH 3450-01 at MWF noon; DES 1200-02 and MATH 1030-07 at MW 09:00; and BIOL 6330-01, the representative of BIOL 5330/6330, at MWF noon. Physics only generalizes its three-credit lectures when the posted slot is MW 15:00. Chairs should distinguish truly fixed sections from ordinary lectures that can use related bell slots.
2. Credit-based recognition misses legitimate instructional arrangements. CHEM 2310/2320 have four credits but meet five times for 50 minutes; CHEM 3300's four-credit lecture meets MWF for 50 minutes plus separate SI. MECH 3600 is four credits at MTWF 09:00, but the engineering importer does not generalize four-credit lectures. Ask which observed meeting patterns may move while retaining their actual contact time; do not derive contact time mechanically from credits when the posted arrangement differs.
3. Lab grids are deliberately narrow and also have boundary omissions. Only 110/170-minute labs on selected day patterns and whole-hour daytime starts generalize. Friday labs, 13:30 starts, MR pairs, and paired-day 170-minute labs stay fixed. The shared grid generation stops before 14:00 for 170-minute labs and before 15:00 for 110-minute labs even though the tagging helper accepts those starts; an 11:00 three-hour phase consequently has only one slot. Examples include BIOL 1615 at 11:00, MTRN 3505 at MW 13:30, and GEOG 3605 on Friday. Review acceptable days, offsets, durations, and latest endings before broadening the grids.
4. BTEC preserves exact times for every hands-on offering, without distinguishing routine movable labs from specialized arrangements. Keep the explicitly fixed BTEC 4050-01A meetings; ask which of BTEC 1015R, 2010, 2030, 3010, and 3050 can move. Titles such as “Scientific Method and Experimental Design” do not contain “lab” and can also bypass lab generalization despite lab-like meetings.
5. Outside-hours meetings, seminars, field sessions, split meetings, and inherited Computing restrictions often remain exact intentionally. A posted early/late meeting does not prove that its section must stay outside core hours. Ask about flexible daytime alternatives separately from permission to teach outside baseline availability. HURCTR travel and linked split components need explicit treatment rather than blanket relaxation.

These findings identify candidates for input changes; the current pass does not broaden imported time domains. The confirmed CS 1410-02 typo is corrected separately below.
